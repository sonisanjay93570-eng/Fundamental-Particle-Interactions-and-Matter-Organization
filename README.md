1. Introduction

This model proposes a process-based approach to studying matter, beginning with fundamental physical interactions and progressing toward the organization of particles, atoms, and material structures.

The central idea is that understanding the visible structure of matter requires investigating its microscopic components, their interactions, energy transfer, and the physical processes through which larger structures form.

2. Conceptual Equation: From Energy to Matter

Energy → Fundamental Particle Interactions → Electrons and Quarks → Photon- and Gluon-Mediated Interactions → Protons and Neutrons → Atoms → Matter

This sequence represents a conceptual framework for organizing research questions. It is not an established chronological sequence of particle creation.

In established physics, electrons and quarks are distinct elementary particles. Photons mediate electromagnetic interactions, while gluons mediate strong interactions between quarks. Protons and neutrons are composite particles made of quarks and gluons.

3. Interaction-Based Relationship

Electron ↔ Photon ↔ Quark ↔ Gluon ↔ Proton / Neutron ↔ Atom ↔ Matter

The double-headed arrows represent possible research connections and relationships between physical concepts. They do not imply that each particle directly transforms into the next.

The relevant physical relationships include:

- Electron and photon: Electromagnetic interactions.
- Quarks and gluons: Strong interactions described by quantum chromodynamics.
- Quarks and nucleons: Quarks and gluon fields contribute to the structure and properties of protons and neutrons.
- Protons, neutrons, and electrons: Their relationships contribute to atomic structure.
- Atoms and matter: Atoms participate in chemical bonding and the organization of material structures.

4. Connection with Action-Flow Theory

Within Sanjay Soni's Action-Flow Theory, the proposed conceptual principle is:

To understand a structure, examine its form, the interactions among its components, the transfer of energy, and the processes that contribute to its organization.

This principle is intended as a research perspective rather than a replacement for established physical theories.

The framework encourages investigation of how interactions produce changes in physical states and how these changes contribute to increasingly organized structures.

5. Scientific Limitations

This model does not establish that:

1. All matter originates through the exact sequence presented in the equations.
2. Electrons directly transform into quarks, protons, or neutrons.
3. Photons and gluons are forms of electric charge.
4. A single unified mechanism has already been demonstrated to explain every stage from fundamental particles to matter.

The proposed relationships require comparison with established physics, precise mathematical definitions, and appropriate experimental evidence.

6. Research Questions

1. How do fundamental interactions contribute to the formation and stability of matter?
2. How do electromagnetic and strong interactions differ and relate?
3. How does energy transfer contribute to changes in physical states?
4. Can a common process-based framework describe relationships across different levels of physical organization without contradicting established theories?
5. Which parts of this conceptual framework can be expressed as testable mathematical predictions?

7. Conclusion

The Fundamental Particle Interactions and Matter Organization model presents a conceptual pathway for studying the relationships between energy, elementary particles, fundamental interactions, atomic structure, and matter.

Its purpose is to organize questions for further investigation, not to claim that an unverified sequence has been scientifically established.
